Before the next interview, an in-house recruitment coordinator needs to check candidate interview status and identify entries that need attention. QueueNote gives that review a defined starting point.

Import the CSV you use to summarise candidate interview statuses, filter the entries by status, and review the follow-up list. Use the list to guide your check against the underlying candidate records and decide what needs attention.

Your spreadsheet is already familiar and requires no new purchase. QueueNote adds a separate import-and-review step, so the useful question is whether its status filters and follow-up list fit your check before the next interview better than the spreadsheet process you use today. There is no measured time-saving claim to rely on yet.

QueueNote currently supports CSV import, status filtering and a follow-up list. It does not connect to an ATS through an API or send emails automatically. Coordinators still need to prepare the CSV and carry out the follow-up work; a list alone does not establish that every omission has been found.

If your HR lead is considering QueueNote, begin with the workflow your recruitment coordinators actually use. Look at what information goes into the CSV, how statuses guide the check before the next interview, and what the coordinator needs to verify before concluding the review.

## Start with the connection your team needs

For a UK recruitment agency with 10–30 people, the first decision is how your candidate-status data reaches the review process. QueueNote currently imports CSV files, filters statuses and provides a follow-up list. TrackDesk currently supports both CSV import and an ATS API connection.

Both products can therefore be considered for a CSV-based workflow. If an ATS API connection is a requirement, QueueNote does not meet that requirement today; TrackDesk's current feature information makes it the option to investigate. Confirm that its API connection supports your specific ATS and the information you need before deciding.

QueueNote Starter costs GBP 8 per seat per month, billed annually at GBP 96 per seat per year, with a minimum of five seats. At five seats, that is GBP 480 per year, equivalent to GBP 40 per month. There are no other mandatory fees in the supplied current price information. Tax treatment is unknown.

For five seats at the stated rates, QueueNote has the lower stated subscription total, but it requires annual payment. TrackDesk uses a workspace price and monthly billing. These figures do not establish which product has the lower total cost for every team: record needs, extra-record charges, tax and the practical work of changing processes must be checked.

If your consultants already work from CSV files, examine QueueNote's status filters and follow-up list against your review task. # Updated English LinkedIn post — event operations

When registrations come through a website form, your event team needs to understand where those submissions go and how long they remain available.

FormRelay saves website-form submissions to CSV for small event organisers in the UK and France. An administrator authorises the first connection. Sync runs are at most 30 minutes apart, and each workspace retains the most recent 7 days of submissions. That sync interval is not a guarantee that every submission arrives within 30 minutes.

A simple way to understand the flow: connect a test form, submit “Demo Guest” for “Workshop A”, and check for that record in the CSV after the next sync. Then check the workspace's 7-day retention explanation.

It supports website forms; telephone and chat registrations are outside this workflow.
